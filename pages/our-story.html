Our Story

How the project came together, where it ran into difficulty, and what we took from it.

Bright Bonds took longer to develop than we anticipated. The original plan was to begin individual matching relatively early, but the reality involved more obstacles.

Our Timeline

January 2025

The Idea

Savannah and I co-founded Bright Bonds as our RISE project for the Bishop Verot Scholars' Academy. We wanted to address social isolation among senior citizens through a program that felt more personal and sustained than a standard volunteer arrangement.

Early 2025

Getting Ready

We wrote a volunteer handbook, compiled an activity and conversation guide, and established the partnership with Shell Point. I built the website during this period as well.

November 2025

First Sessions

Sessions began at Shell Point in November 2025. We held group sessions rather than moving immediately to individual matching, with the goal of building familiarity and identifying participants who could commit to a regular schedule.

Late 2025

Slower Than Expected

Sustaining participation was the most difficult part of the process. Students had scheduling conflicts, and building interest on both sides took longer than anticipated. Matching was delayed as a result. We continued meeting and adjusted our approach along the way.

2025–2026

Congressional App Challenge

We submitted the Bright Bonds website to the Congressional App Challenge for FL-19 (Rep. Byron Donalds) and placed runner-up. We were subsequently invited to meet with Congressman Donalds.

March 2026

Matching

After many group sessions, we began a pilot program and matched 5 individual pairs based on shared interests. The timeline was longer than we originally planned, but these matches are what the program was designed to produce.

Ongoing

Next Year

The program is still active. We plan to continue in the 2026–2027 school year, invest more time in the existing pairs, and expand participation on both sides.

What We Learned

We didn't anticipate how much the project would be affected by logistics. Bringing students and residents together consistently was harder than expected, and it pushed back the matching timeline. These challenges, however, taught us a lot about communication and planning.

We kept working together, eventually creating a pilot program with 5 pairs. The program is still running, and Savannah and I plan to use what we've learned to continue expanding next year.
