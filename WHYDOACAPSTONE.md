WHY DO A CAPSTONE PROJECT?
BISHOP VEROT SCHOLARS ACADEMY

College Admissions officers want to see sustained dedication to something students care about. Instead of following the passions of others, explore what makes you excited. By turning your interests into impact, you can create a standout resume allowing you to compete with other top applicants vying for admission to our country’s most competitive colleges and universities alongside you. Consider these ideas to spark your own...

PROBLEM SOLVING
The University of Florida’s Dr. Peter Sayeski said he likes to see how an applicant has figured out a unique solution to an issue or obstacle. One applicant noticed a shoe shortage when he was traveling abroad. When he returned home, he created a shoe drive for underprivileged community members. “Based on an experience that he acquired overseas, he was able to connect that to a solution and grew something from nothing to help serve his community.”

EXPANDING ON AN INTEREST
Passion projects can also be about adding layers or new dimensions to activities you do inside or outside of school. One student played tennis in high school and later became the team captain. During the summers, he would also teach lessons for free. Dr. Sayeski says they want to see “the growth of students and how they can continue to evolve their interests.”

PERSPECTIVE
Dr. Claire Phipps at SUNY Polytechnic Institute says they are looking for students with an “other-oriented perspective.” In other words, they are looking for students who are volunteering not because it is a requirement for the National Honor Society, but because they genuinely want to help others.

TURN PARTICIPATION INTO IMPACT
Simply being inducted into the National Honor Society and volunteering once a month to earn hours is not enough to impress admissions committees at top schools—students must use their participation as a vehicle for making impactful change in their communities if they want to stand out. NHS leaders can use their membership as an incubator for a unique passion project or a standout community initiative.

BUILD COMMUNITY
Nidhi Bhaskar, a first-year medical student at the Warren Alpert Medical School of Brown University trained as a first responder through the Community Emergency Response Team (CERT) of FEMA (Federal Emergency Management Agency). Bhaskar leveraged this experience to create a similar program at her high school and five other schools to train fellow students about first aid and emergency preparedness.

SERVICE LEADERSHIP
“What we are interested in is how you are serving your community. The advice we give students is to find what ignites their passion and then do it at the highest level possible, at whatever level they are capable of.” - Professor Hamilton of the New Jersey Institute of Technology

TEAMWORK
Past projects included a student who produced a set of braille blocks and a game partnered after Scrabble called “Brabble” for children who are visually impaired or blind. Other projects include making artificial arms and hands for children born without them. One student developed golf clubs for veterans who had lost fingers. “All of these activities are unique because it’s a multi-disciplinary team working together,” Dr. Phipps said.
